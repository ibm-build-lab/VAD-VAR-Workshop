## 1. Objectives

This lab demonstrates the role Watson Discovery plays in the retrieval augmented generation (RAG) pattern. You will use Watson Discovery as a data ingestion and storage layer and search algorithm to extract relevant passages from documents. Those search results will then be used as an input context to a watsonx large language model (LLM) to generate a more trustworthy and date-correct completion. The watsonx LLM generates a natural language answer to the query based on the documents provided by the search.

---

## 2. High-level RAG architecture

RAG is one of the most common generative AI architecture patterns. One obvious use case for RAG is search and question-answering (Q&A) systems because of RAG’s ability to use a trusted corpus to aid a large language model (LLM) in its completion. For example, building systems that leverage LLMs to distill and make sense of information from their repositories, enabling Q&A over documents. RAG helps to add guardrails and ground an LLM to a truth-grounded corpus to help prevent hallucination.

Generally, a RAG pattern consists of two steps:

- **Search / Retrieval**: Extracting relevant text snippets from trusted enterprise knowledge bases.
- **Summarization / Generation**: Generating answers to the question based on the context provided by the Search / Retrieval step. Typically, passages and snippets are concatenated and appended to the prompt.

The following is a general high-level RAG architecture diagram and shows how watsonx.ai works. There can be many variations to this architecture (such as integration with watsonx Assistant, watsonx Orchestrate, or other applications clients are using) depending on the target use case. This diagram simply shows two of the most common RAG patterns.

## 7. General recommendations and suggestions

Here are some suggestions:

1. **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)** provides one of the best ways to reduce (but not eliminate) foundation model hallucination. You should test out and become familiar with varying the contents before trying it for the first time in front of the client:
   - a. Use different foundation models.
   - b. Change the config parameters such as `max_tokens`, `min_tokens`, and `temperature`.
   - c. This lab notebook does not go into Sampling decoding mode. You can also try this approach (instructions and information are available in the watsonx.ai Technical Sales Level 3 lab).

2. You can easily customize this lab and create a collection of a client’s data files. Keep in mind that **the better the data quality, the better the results**. You should ask your client for curated, validated data sets — not just a random collection.

3. Focus on the **objective** of the PoX and show how RAG can be used to generate answers from client documents versus being able to handle complex and different prompts.
