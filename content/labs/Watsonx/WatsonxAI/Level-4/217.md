# InstructLab: Tuning IBM Granite AI foundation model for enterprise use cases using InstructLab on watsonx.ai

The rapid evolution of generative AI has highlighted this vital truth: general purpose models, though powerful, frequently fall short when it comes to addressing the precise, nuanced needs of enterprise applications. Enterprises face a myriad of pain points today: handling industry-specific jargon, compliance requirements, addressing security concerns, and achieving reliable performance at scale.

Model customization addresses these pain points by enabling the creation of domain-specific language models that deeply understand unique terminologies, data contexts, and regulatory landscapes. These specialized models not only enhance the accuracy and responsiveness of the models but also empower businesses to implement tailored solutions that directly target their operational challenges, which ultimately paves the way for true generative AI success in production environments.


InstructLab, a collaborative initiative between IBM Research and Red Hat, provides a cost-effective approach to enhance AI model alignment. Its core methodology, [**LAB (Large-scale Alignment for chatBots)**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01081), comprises three key components: taxonomy-driven data curation, large-scale synthetic data generation, and multi-phased instruction-tuning. Together, these processes make InstructLab a powerful tool for optimizing large language models with efficiency and precision, setting the stage for transformative AI applications in enterprise environments. You will understand more details about each step, and will experience them in action in this tutorial.

This tutorial serves as a practical, hands-on guide designed to walk you through the alignment tuning process for IBM’s Granite fit-for-purpose AI language model using InstructLab on watsonx.ai. By the end of the tutorial, you'll understand how fine-tuning on targeted regulatory data can transform a general-purpose model into a specialized compliance tool, perfectly tailored to meet stringent industry-specific requirements.

### Why use InstructLab on watsonx.ai?

InstructLab on watsonx.ai democratizes AI model customization, empowering organizations to tailor large-scale language models to their specific operational needs. Integrated within the [**Tuning Studio of watsonx.ai**](https://dataplatform.cloud.ibm.com/docs/content/wsj/analyze-data/fm-tuning-studio.html?context=wx&locale=en), InstructLab offers AI developers, data scientists, and subject matter experts an intuitive, full-scale alignment-tuning experience. This includes data lineage and evaluation features, alongside robust computational infrastructure.

This seamless integration of InstructLab within watsonx.ai bridges the gap between affordable, accessible training and full-scale enterprise deployment. It allows businesses to initiate projects on a small scale and expand as their AI requirements evolve. Furthermore, all customized models can be governed within watsonx.governance on the same platform.

With InstructLab, companies gain a powerful tool that not only comprehends their specific use cases but also adapts and evolves with their dynamic AI strategies. This ultimately drives enhanced model performance, productivity, and adherence to industry standards.

### Use Case: Compliance solution for AI-driven financial products

In today’s rapidly evolving generative AI landscape, the finance industry must remain at the forefront. Banks and financial institutions are actively launching innovative AI-driven products, such as dynamic mortgages and AI-assisted investment tools, presenting both opportunities and challenges. One forward-thinking EU financial organization encountered a critical challenge: ensuring their cutting-edge AI-driven financial products complied with the stringent standards of the [**EU AI Act**](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689).

This tutorial's use case demonstrates how the IBM Granite model can be leveraged to create an effective compliance solution through a practical, hands-on approach.

### How the AI-driven compliance solution works

Our AI-driven solution operates in two steps:

1.  **Input the product description:** The product team enters detailed information about a new AI-powered mortgage product, describing its dynamic interest rate adjustments influenced by customer behavior, market conditions, and predictive models.
2.  **AI compliance analysis:** A specialized AI agent, fine-tuned on the latest European AI Act revisions via InstructLab, reviews the submitted product details. This intelligent analysis agent performs the following:
    * Scans for potential risks.
    * Checks the product against key regulatory provisions such as transparency, fairness, and explainability, and provides remediation steps.

### Steps

This tutorial guides you step by step in fine-tuning the IBM Granite model using InstructLab on watsonx.ai to create a specialized compliance tool for financial services, specifically for ensuring financial products adhere to the European AI Act.

These are the high-level steps you will follow:

- Step 1: Create a Secrets Manager service: Create a Secrets Manager service on IBM Cloud to store GitHub credentials securely.
- Step 2: Access watsonx.ai and create a project: Access the watsonx.ai Studio, create a project, and associate it with Cloud Object Storage and watsonx.ai Runtime services.
- Step 3: Create a tuning experiment and review the taxonomy: Create a tuning experiment, review the taxonomy of the base IBM Granite model, and configure InstructLab on watsonx.ai.
- Step 4: Create the taxonomy and configure GitHub access: Create a taxonomy on GitHub, configure access to it, and store the personal access token securely.
- Step 5: Add knowledge or skills to the taxonomy: Add data and facts to the taxonomy through creating seed examples and uploading grounded data.
- Step 6: Synthetic data generation: Generate synthetic data based on the taxonomy seed examples, and grounded data.
- Step 7: Multi-phase tuning: Tune the Granite model using the generated synthetic data in a two-phase process (knowledge tuning and skills tuning).
- Step 8: Benchmarking: Evaluate the performance of the tuned model using Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU) and Multi-turn benchmark (MT-Bench) benchmarks.
- Step 9: Deployment: Deploy the customized model on watsonx.ai, making it available for inference.
- Step 10: Inference on watsonx.ai Prompt Lab: Test the customized model on Prompt Lab, observing its understanding of the latest EU AI Act regulations and its ability to identify compliance gaps and provide remediation steps.

#### Step 7: Multi-phase tuning

Given the satisfactory synthetic data generated in the previous step, you can now proceed with tuning the model using this data.

InstructLab on watsonx.ai employs a two-phase tuning process to incrementally enhance the model's performance while maintaining training stability:

* **Knowledge Tuning:** This phase focuses on integrating new factual information. It is structured to first train the model on short responses and then extend to long responses and foundational skills.
* **Skills Tuning:** Building upon the knowledge phase, this stage enhances the
model's ability to apply its knowledge across diverse tasks and contexts, with particular attention to developing compositional skills.

Each phase involves multiple epochs to ensure training quality. Research benchmarks are used to select the optimal model checkpoint at each phase, and a replay buffer of the synthetic data is maintained to prevent catastrophic forgetting, ensuring continuous learning and adaptation.

#### Step 8: Benchmarking

In this step, you will evaluate the performance of the tuned model based on two widely recognized benchmarks: **Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU)** and **Multi-turn benchmark (MT-Bench)**.

**Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU)** is a benchmark designed to assess the extent of a language model's knowledge across a broad spectrum of academic and professional subjects. It goes beyond evaluating mere language proficiency, also testing problem-solving skills in areas encompassing STEM, humanities, and social sciences. MMLU's diverse range of subjects and question types tests both factual recall and problem-solving capabilities. This makes it an ideal benchmark for gauging how much a model has "learned" from its training data and how effectively it generalizes to new domains and subjects not directly covered during training.

The **Multi-turn (MT) Benchmark** is specifically designed to evaluate a model's proficiency in performing custom skills as defined by the InstructLab taxonomy. Unlike standardized benchmarks targeting general knowledge, MT focuses on specialized capabilities tailored to specific needs or domains. The evaluation process is enhanced by utilizing both teacher and judge models to ensure the quality and relevance of the assessment. It assesses the model on custom skills added to the ilab taxonomy. The teacher model generates question/answer pairs based on seed qna pairs provided by the user, and the judge model evaluates the model's answer using the user-provided seed questions and answers as a reference.

1.  Click **Performance** to explore the results of the **Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU)** and **Multi-turn benchmark (MT-Bench)**.
   ![](<../images/217/Screenshot 2025-05-04 at 1.06.31 AM.png>)
    
    The page will display the MMLU and MT-Bench scores. The MMLU score ranges from 0 to 1 for the knowledge tuning benchmark, while the MT-Bench score ranges from 0 to 10 for skills tuning. When you have a base model and train or "tune" it on additional data (e.g., extra knowledge documents), you want to see how much that additional training has improved performance. MMLU helps quantify this improvement by showing:
      * **Baseline Score:** The initial score of your base model on the MMLU benchmark.
      * **Tuned Score:** The score of your tuned model, which may have been trained on added domain knowledge.
      * **Comparison:** By comparing the baseline score with the tuned score, you can determine if the additional training genuinely enhanced the model’s ability to answer questions correctly across multiple subjects.
2.  You can click the “eye” icon next to each benchmark for more detailed insights. This provides a granular view of the improvements achieved in the model's domain-specific expertise. Each score metric offers insight into which areas have benefited most from further training, enabling developers to target further refinements.
   ![](<../images/217/Screenshot 2025-05-04 at 1.06.43 AM.png>)
